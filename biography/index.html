Right now: taping out a trajectory reconstruction accelerator on TSMC 28nm.

I currently conduct research in the Parallel Computing System Laboratory (PCS Lab) under Prof. Bo-Cheng Lai. My thesis builds on traccc, the GPU track-reconstruction demonstrator of the ACTS project, in collaboration with the University of Washington and CERN. I am also second and corresponding author of UniTrac, an FPGA accelerator for GNN-based track reconstruction, co-developed with UW and UIUC.

For this thesis I built and benchmarked, on my own, a design-space exploration across several accelerator architectures, aimed at the throughput the High-Luminosity LHC's high-level trigger demands. On GPUs, I introduced event-level fusion to eliminate the host-launch bottleneck, only to uncover work-bound kernels crippled by data-dependent warp divergence. Porting the design to the spatial fabric of the AMD Versal V80 removed the divergence, but limited routing resources capped the core count, and the feedback loop inherent to the combinatorial Kalman filter set a hard bound on iteration throughput. MIMD manycore architectures such as Tenstorrent avoided divergence as well, but at the cost of compute density.

Each approach converged on the same conclusion: this problem demands a custom accelerator whose architecture and compiler are co-designed around irregular memory access. That is the system I am now building in RTL, on the way to a TSMC 28nm tapeout.
