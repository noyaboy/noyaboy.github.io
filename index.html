Right now: making trigger-level particle tracking keep pace with 40 million beam crossings a second.

I work on the hardware side of machine learning in the Parallel Computing System Laboratory, advised by Prof. Bo-Cheng Lai, and collaborate with researchers at the University of Washington and UIUC on real-time machine learning for high-energy physics.

My current focus is traccc, the ACTS project’s GPU track-reconstruction demonstrator; my goal is to make it fast enough for the High-Luminosity LHC’s high-level trigger — a system that lives or dies on throughput. I optimize its Kalman-filter tracking at the CUDA-kernel level, profile the pipeline with Nsight Systems and Compute, and am building a high-level synthesis (HLS) accelerator for it on the AMD Versal V80. In the long term, I aim to pursue a Ph.D. on the codesign of ML architectures and compilers.

UniTrac: A Unified FPGA Accelerator for GNN-based Particle Track Reconstruction in HEPs

Yun-Chen Yang, Hao-Chun Liang, Hsuan-Wei Yu, Edwin Arkel Rios, Santosh Parajuli, Mark S. Neubauer, Shih-Chieh Hsu, Bo-Cheng Lai

ACM Transactions on Reconfigurable Technology and Systems, 2025 in submission Corresponding author

One FPGA accelerator that runs the entire GNN tracking pipeline — graph construction, GNN inference, and track labeling — within the real-time budgets of the HL-LHC.

Abstract

Trajectory reconstruction in high-energy physics, particularly for the High-Luminosity Large Hadron Collider (HL-LHC), presents a significant computational challenge due to the immense pile-up and data rates. This paper presents a unified Field-Programmable Gate Array (FPGA) accelerator of a complete Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based pipeline for particle tracking. By leveraging the parallel processing capabilities of FPGAs, we propose an efficient hardware architecture that accelerates the GNN inference process. The design optimizes both latency and throughput, ensuring that the trajectory reconstruction meets the stringent real-time requirements of the HL-LHC experiments. Our implementation demonstrates the feasibility and performance benefits of using FPGA-accelerated GNNs for next-generation particle detector trigger systems.

Real-Time GPU Kalman-Filter Tracking via Kernel Refactoring and INT8 Surrogates for High-Luminosity Colliders

Hao-Chun Liang, Yuan-Tang Chou, Bo-Cheng Lai

Fast Machine Learning for Science Conference, ETH Zurich, 2025 First author

Kernel refactoring plus INT8 surrogates speed up GPU Kalman-filter track fitting by 2.86× end to end — closing in on the HL-LHC trigger’s real-time budget.

Poster Photo
Abstract

As the era of the High-Luminosity Large Hadron Collider (HL-LHC) approaches, the GPU-accelerated High-Level Trigger (HLT) of the CMS experiment faces a stringent requirement to reduce the Level-1 readout stream from 100 kHz to 5 kHz, a twenty-fold decrease essential to adhere to archival bandwidth constraints. Meeting this demand necessitates highly efficient real-time charged-particle tracking. In the recent release of the ACTS Traccc pipeline, the Kalman-filter Fit kernel has emerged as the predominant latency bottleneck, primarily due to excessive register pressure and serialization of matrix inversion operations within GPU warp execution. To address these performance limitations, we propose two synergistic GPU optimizations: a kernel refactoring method that restructures the Fit kernel into three distinct computational phases — Predict, Update, and Finalize — each delineated by a single __syncthreads() barrier, enabling more efficient compiler-driven register allocation and reduced spill traffic; and a substitution of the analytic matrix inversion with a quantization-aware INT8 multilayer perceptron trained to approximate the 6×2 Kalman-gain matrix, executed largely in registers via NVIDIA’s __dp4a instruction. Evaluations on Geant4-simulated Open-Data-Detector events show that kernel refactoring alone improves throughput by roughly 15%, while the MLP surrogate replicates the analytic Kalman-gain matrix with a mean-squared error below 8 × 10⁻⁵. On an NVIDIA RTX 2080 Ti, the combined optimizations improve Fit-kernel reciprocal throughput by 5.22× (11.5 ms to 2.2 ms) and the end-to-end pipeline from 23.5 ms to 8.23 ms — an overall speed-up of 2.86× — transitioning execution from memory- and SFU-bound to predominantly compute-bound, and approaching the computational-latency budget imposed by the HL-LHC HLT.

Fine-Grained Image Recognition from Scratch with Teacher-Guided Data Augmentation

Edwin Arkel Rios, Fernando Mikael, Oswin Gosal, Femiloye Oyerinde, Hao-Chun Liang, Bo-Cheng Lai, Min-Chun Hu

arXiv:2507.12157, 2025

Teacher-guided data augmentation trains fine-grained recognition models from scratch — no ImageNet pretraining — matching pretrained baselines at a fraction of the compute.

arXiv PDF
Abstract

Fine-grained image recognition (FGIR) aims to distinguish visually similar sub-categories within a broader class, such as identifying bird species. While most existing FGIR methods rely on backbones pretrained on large-scale datasets like ImageNet, this dependence limits adaptability to resource-constrained environments and hinders the development of task-specific architectures tailored to the unique challenges of FGIR. In this work, we challenge the conventional reliance on pretrained models by demonstrating that high-performance FGIR systems can be trained entirely from scratch. We introduce a novel training framework, TGDA, that integrates data-aware augmentation with weak supervision via a fine-grained-aware teacher model, implemented through knowledge distillation. This framework unlocks the design of task-specific and hardware-aware architectures, including LRNets for low-resolution FGIR and ViTFS, a family of Vision Transformers optimized for efficient inference. Extensive experiments across three FGIR benchmarks over diverse settings involving low-resolution and high-resolution inputs show that our method consistently matches or surpasses state-of-the-art pretrained counterparts. In particular, in the low-resolution setting, LRNets trained with TGDA improve accuracy by up to 23% over prior methods while requiring up to 20.6× less parameters, lower FLOPs, and significantly less training data. Similarly, ViTFS-T can match the performance of a ViT B-16 pretrained on ImageNet-21k while using 15.3× fewer trainable parameters and requiring orders of magnitudes less data. These results highlight TGDA’s potential as an adaptable alternative to pretraining, paving the way for more efficient fine-grained vision systems.
