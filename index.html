Hao-Chun Liang

Right now: taping out a trajectory reconstruction accelerator on TSMC 28nm.

I graduated with distinction in Electrical Engineering from National Tsing Hua University, ranking in the top 5% of my class with a GPA of 4.1/4.3.

I currently conduct research in the Parallel Computing System Laboratory (PCS Lab) under Prof. Bo-Cheng Lai. My thesis centers on traccc, the GPU-based track-reconstruction demonstrator for the ACTS project, developed in collaboration with the University of Washington and CERN. I am also the second and corresponding author of UniTrac, an FPGA accelerator for GNN-based track reconstruction, co-developed with UW and UIUC.

Through this thesis, I independently designed and benchmarked a design-space exploration spanning multiple accelerator architectures, targeting the throughput demands of the High-Luminosity LHC's high-level trigger. On GPUs, I introduced event-level fusion to eliminate the host-launch bottleneck—only to uncover work-bound kernels crippled by data-dependent warp divergence. Porting the design to the AMD Versal V80's spatial fabric eliminated this divergence, but limited routing resources capped achievable core count, while the feedback loop inherent to the combinatorial Kalman filter imposed a hard bound on iteration throughput. Shifting to MIMD manycore architectures such as Tenstorrent again avoided divergence, but at the cost of compute density.

Each approach converged on the same conclusion: this problem demands a custom accelerator whose architecture and compiler are co-designed around irregular memory access. That is the system I am now building in RTL, en route to a TSMC 28nm tapeout.
