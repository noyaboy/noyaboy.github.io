UniTrac: A Unified FPGA Accelerator for GNN-based Particle Track Reconstruction in HEPs

Yun-Chen Yang, Hao-Chun Liang*, Hsuan-Wei Yu, Edwin Arkel Rios, Santosh Parajuli, Mark S. Neubauer, Shih-Chieh Hsu, Bo-Cheng Lai

ACM Transactions on Reconfigurable Technology and Systems, 2025 In submission

Abstract

Trajectory reconstruction in high-energy physics, particularly for the High-Luminosity Large Hadron Collider (HL-LHC), presents a significant computational challenge due to the immense pile-up and data rates. This paper presents a unified Field-Programmable Gate Array (FPGA) accelerator of a complete Graph Neural Network (GNN)-based pipeline for particle tracking. By leveraging the parallel processing capabilities of FPGAs, we propose an efficient hardware architecture that accelerates the GNN inference process. The design optimizes both latency and throughput, ensuring that the trajectory reconstruction meets the stringent real-time requirements of the HL-LHC experiments. Our implementation demonstrates the feasibility and performance benefits of using FPGA-accelerated GNNs for next-generation particle detector trigger systems.

A High-Performance Implementation of GNN-Based Trajectory Reconstruction on FPGA

Yun-Chen Yang, Hao-Chun Liang, Hsuan-Wei Yu, Bo-Cheng Lai, Shih-Chieh Hsu, Mark Neubauer, Santosh Pandey

VLSI Design / CAD Symposium, 2025

Paper Slides Photo
Abstract

We propose a high-performance hardware implementation for GNN-based trajectory reconstruction, tailored for the computing demands of the High-Luminosity LHC. This work focuses on optimizing the hardware mapping of Graph Neural Networks onto FPGA platforms to maximize throughput and minimize latency. The proposed architecture, referred to as HiGTR, employs efficient dataflow management and processing element utilization to handle the sparse and irregular nature of graph data in particle tracking. Experimental results indicate that our FPGA-based solution significantly exceeds the baseline latency and throughput requirements, providing a robust and scalable solution for real-time edge processing in high-energy physics applications.

Real-Time GPU Kalman-Filter Tracking via Kernel Refactoring and INT8 Surrogates for High-Luminosity Colliders

Hao-Chun Liang, Yuan-Tang Chou, Bo-Cheng Lai

Fast Machine Learning for Science Conference, CERN (hybrid), 2025

Poster Photo
Abstract

Standard Kalman Filter-based tracking algorithms are computationally intensive and struggle to scale with the high-luminosity conditions expected at future colliders. This work presents a real-time GPU-accelerated tracking approach that leverages kernel refactoring and low-precision INT8 surrogates to enhance performance. By optimizing memory access patterns and utilizing reduced-precision arithmetic, we achieve a substantial speedup in tracking throughput without compromising physics accuracy. The proposed method effectively utilizes the massive parallelism of modern GPUs, demonstrating its potential as a viable solution for the online trigger level tracking at the HL-LHC.

Fine-Grained Image Recognition from Scratch with Teacher-Guided Data Augmentation

Edwin Arkel Rios, Fernando Mikael, Oswin Gosal, Femiloye Oyerinde, Hao-Chun Liang, Bo-Cheng Lai, Min-Chun Hu

arXiv, 2025

Paper
Abstract

Fine-grained image recognition (FGIR) aims to distinguish visually similar sub-categories within a broader class. While most existing FGIR methods rely on backbones pretrained on large-scale datasets like ImageNet, this dependence limits adaptability to resource-constrained environments and hinders the development of task-specific architectures. In this work, we challenge the conventional reliance on pretrained models by introducing a novel training framework called Teacher-Guided Data Augmentation (TGDA). TGDA integrates data-aware augmentation with weak supervision via a fine-grained-aware teacher model, implemented through knowledge distillation. This framework enables the training of high-performance FGIR systems entirely from scratch, unlocking the design of task-specific architectures such as LRNets for low-resolution FGIR and ViTFS, a family of Vision Transformers optimized for efficient inference. Extensive experiments across three FGIR benchmarks show that our method matches or surpasses state-of-the-art pretrained counterparts, improving accuracy by up to 23% in low-resolution settings while significantly reducing parameters and computational costs.
