  • Trajectory Reconstruction Accelerator TSMC 28nm Tapeout (Parallel Computing System Laboratory, NYCU, Jul 2026 - Present) Taking the trajectory reconstruction accelerator from RTL to silicon through a TSMC 28nm tapeout. The design grew out of my thesis's design-space exploration on traccc. On GPUs, event-level fusion eliminated the host-launch bottleneck but exposed work-bound kernels crippled by data-dependent warp divergence. The spatial fabric of the AMD Versal V80 removed the divergence, but limited routing resources capped the core count, and the feedback loop of the combinatorial Kalman filter bounded iteration throughput. MIMD manycores such as Tenstorrent avoided divergence at the cost of compute density. These trade-offs pointed to a custom accelerator whose architecture and compiler are co-designed around irregular memory access.
  • AI Agent Automation Flow (Andes Technology, Jul 2026 - Present) Automates the team's workflow with custom SKILL.md files across four stages: mental-model extraction, gap analysis, execution and knowledge management. Slides
  • Regfile RTL/C Code Generation Flow (Andes Technology, Nov 2024 - Aug 2025; Jul 2026 - Nov 2026) An automation flow that compiles CSV register specifications into synthesizable Verilog register files, C/C++ headers, and documentation, keeping the hardware and software interfaces consistent. Extended in 2026 with intermediate-representation (IR) optimization. Tools: Perl, Python, Verilog, Shell, Makefile.
  • Dual-Core RISC CPU: From RTL to Signoff in UMC 180nm (ICLAB Final Project, NYCU, Mar 2026 - Jun 2026) A dual-core RISC processor carried through the complete RTL-to-GDSII flow in UMC 180nm, spanning design, simulation and formal verification, synthesis, timing closure, and place-and-route. Tools: Verilog, VCS, SpyGlass, Design Compiler, JasperGold, PrimeTime, Innovus. GitHub
